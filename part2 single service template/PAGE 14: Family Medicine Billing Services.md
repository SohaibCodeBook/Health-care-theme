 H2: Family medicine billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is family medicine billing?
 Family medicine billing is the coding and claims process for primary care practices, covering E/M visits, preventive care, vaccine administration, and chronic care management programs. It requires accurate visit leveling and capturing recurring revenue programs beyond the standard office visit.
Q2: Why should family medicine practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing family medicine billing gives your practice specialists in E/M leveling, preventive visit rules, and chronic care programs. Because primary care runs on volume, small per-visit coding losses add up fast, and specialized billing recovers revenue while your staff focuses on patients.
Q3: How much do family medicine billing services cost?
 Family medicine billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections, with high-volume practices often getting favorable rates. Because chronic care management and annual wellness visits are commonly under-captured, recovered revenue often exceeds the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: Can you bill a preventive visit and a problem visit on the same day?
 Yes, when documentation supports a separately identifiable problem addressed during a preventive visit, using modifier 25 to bill both. Many family medicine practices give away problem visits inside physicals because this modifier is not applied or documented correctly.
Q5: What is chronic care management billing?
 Chronic care management is a Medicare program that reimburses monthly for care coordination work performed for patients with two or more chronic conditions, including care plan updates and between-visit communication. Most family medicine practices already do this work but never bill for it.
Q6: Why do family medicine claims get denied?
 Family medicine claims are most often denied for E/M level mismatches with documentation, missing modifier 25 on combined preventive and problem visits, and vaccine administration billed without required components. High patient volume makes these small errors add up quickly across a panel.
