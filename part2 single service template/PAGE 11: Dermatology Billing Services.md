 H2: Dermatology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is dermatology billing?
 Dermatology billing is the coding and claims process for skin procedures, covering lesion excisions, biopsies, destructions, and Mohs surgery. It requires precise coding by lesion size and anatomic site, correct multiple procedure sequencing, and a clear separation between medically necessary and cosmetic services.
Q2: Why should dermatology practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing dermatology billing gives your practice specialists in lesion coding, excision rules, and same-day procedure modifiers. Because dermatology performs multiple procedures per visit at high volume, specialized billing captures the units, sizes, and modifiers that general billers routinely code down or miss.
Q3: How much do dermatology billing services cost?
 Dermatology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections, reflecting the high procedure volume dermatology practices generate. Because undermeasured lesions and modifier errors are common, specialized billing usually recovers well beyond the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: How are skin lesion removals coded?
 Lesion excisions and destructions are coded by size including margins, anatomic site, and whether the pathology result is benign or malignant, each following a different code family. Undermeasured lesions and wrong site categories are among the most common dermatology billing errors.
Q5: Can you bill an office visit and a procedure on the same day?
 Yes, when documentation supports a separately identifiable evaluation beyond the procedure itself, using modifier 25. This modifier is heavily audited in dermatology, so the visit and the procedure both need clear, distinct documentation to support payment for each.
Q6: How is Mohs surgery billed?
 Mohs surgery is billed by stage and tissue block, with strict documentation requirements at each stage, and any reconstructive repair billed separately with correct sequencing. Incomplete stage or block documentation is a common reason Mohs claims underpay relative to the work performed.
