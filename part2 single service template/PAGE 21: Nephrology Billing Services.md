 H2: Nephrology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is nephrology billing?
 Nephrology billing is the coding and claims process for kidney care practices, covering monthly capitation dialysis codes, CKD stage diagnosis coding, and hospital rounding claims. It requires reconciling patient visits across office, hospital, and dialysis facility settings, since nephrologists bill across more care settings than most specialties.
Q2: Why should nephrology practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing nephrology billing gives your practice specialists in monthly capitation codes, multi-setting claim reconciliation, and CKD diagnosis coding. Because nephrologists work across offices, hospitals, and dialysis units, specialized billing captures the charges that fall between settings in generic operations.
Q3: How much do nephrology billing services cost?
 Nephrology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections. Because MCP visit tier errors and missed hospital rounding charges are common across multi-setting practices, recovered revenue often exceeds the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: How does monthly capitation dialysis billing work?
 Medicare pays dialysis management through monthly capitation codes tiered by the number of face-to-face visits delivered that month, meaning the same patient can generate different reimbursement levels depending on documented visit frequency. Reconciling documented visits against the tier billed captures the highest supported payment.
Q5: Can nephrologists bill for home dialysis management?
 Yes. Home dialysis monthly management and patient training follow their own code set and rules, separate from in-center dialysis billing. As more patients move to home modalities, capturing this revenue stream correctly matters more every year.
Q6: Why do nephrology claims get denied?
 Nephrology claims are most often denied for MCP visit tier mismatches, missing hospital rounding charges, and CKD stage diagnosis codes that do not support billed services. Because nephrology spans so many care settings, reconciling documented visits against submitted charges prevents most of these gaps.
