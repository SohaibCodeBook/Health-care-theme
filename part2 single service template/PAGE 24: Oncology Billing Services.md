 H2: Oncology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is oncology billing?
 Oncology billing is the coding and claims process for cancer treatment practices, covering chemotherapy administration hierarchies, J-code drug units, and treatment authorizations. It requires extreme unit accuracy since drug claims dominate oncology revenue at some of the highest dollar values in medicine.
Q2: Why should oncology practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing oncology billing gives your practice specialists in chemotherapy coding, J-code accuracy, and treatment authorizations. Because drug claims dominate oncology revenue at extreme dollar values, specialized billing prevents the unit and wastage errors that can cost more than a full day of visits.
Q3: How much do oncology billing services cost?
 Oncology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections, often with rates reflecting the complexity of drug billing. Because a single J-code unit error can cost thousands, specialized billing usually recovers well beyond its cost. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: How is drug wastage billed in oncology?
 Discarded drug amounts from single-dose vials are billed with the JW modifier under each payer's wastage policy, representing legitimately billable revenue that undocumented workflows often forfeit. Payer audits increasingly focus on wastage documentation, so it needs to be both captured and compliant.
Q5: Do chemotherapy regimens require prior authorization?
 Yes. Prior authorization is required for essentially every chemotherapy and immunotherapy regimen before treatment starts, and renewals are needed through regimen changes. A denied authorization on one infusion can mean a five-figure unrecoverable loss given the cost of oncology drugs.
Q6: Why do oncology claims get denied?
 Oncology claims are most often denied for J-code unit conversion errors, missing or lapsed treatment authorizations, and drug wastage documentation gaps. Because a units error in either direction is serious in oncology, causing major underpayment or compliance exposure, every drug line needs dedicated verification.
