 H2: Neurosurgery billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is neurosurgery billing?
 Neurosurgery billing is the coding and claims process for neurosurgical practices, covering multi-level spine procedures, cranial and tumor cases, and co-surgeon claims. It requires capturing every billable component from complex operative reports, since neurosurgical claims carry the highest dollar values in professional billing.
Q2: Why should neurosurgical practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing neurosurgery billing gives your practice coders trained in multi-level spine cases, co-surgeon rules, and complex operative reports. Because neurosurgical claims carry the highest values in professional billing, a single missed component or level costs more than most specialties lose in a month.
Q3: How much do neurosurgery billing services cost?

Given the extreme dollar value of neurosurgical claims, even small percentage improvements in capture translate to significant recovered revenue. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: How is multi-level spine surgery coded?
 Multi-level spine surgery is coded by capturing every documented level, including the primary procedure plus add-on codes for additional levels, instrumentation, and bone graft work. Add-on level omissions are the biggest revenue leak in spine billing.
Q5: Can two surgeons bill for the same neurosurgical procedure?
 Yes, when two surgeons perform distinct parts of one procedure, commonly in anterior spine approaches, the case is billed with modifier 62 for co-surgeons, subject to each payer's eligibility rules for the specific procedure. Assistant surgeon cases use modifier 80 instead.
Q6: Why do neurosurgery claims get denied or underpaid?
 Neurosurgery claims are most often denied or underpaid for missed add-on spine levels, incorrect co-surgeon modifiers, global period billing errors, and payer reimbursement that falls short of contracted rates. Given the size of these claims, even small percentage shortfalls represent significant dollars.
