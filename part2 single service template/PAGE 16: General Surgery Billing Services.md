 H2: General surgery billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is general surgery billing?
 General surgery billing is the coding and claims process for surgical practices, covering operative reports, global period tracking, and assistant or co-surgeon claims. It requires precise modifier application to capture staged procedures, returns to the OR, and multiple procedures performed in a single operation.
Q2: Why should surgical practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing general surgery billing gives your practice coders trained in operative reports, global periods, and surgical modifiers. Because surgical claims carry high dollar values and strict payer edits, a small error rate costs far more than in visit-based specialties, and specialized coding protects that revenue.
Q3: How much do general surgery billing services cost?
 General surgery billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections, reflecting the high value of surgical claims. Because global period and modifier errors are common and costly, specialized billing usually recovers more than it costs. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: What is a global period in surgical billing?
 A global period is the 90 day window after major surgery during which routine post-op care is included in the surgical payment, but unrelated visits, staged procedures, and returns to the OR remain separately billable with the correct modifiers. Global period confusion is the top surgical revenue leak.
Q5: Can you bill for assistant surgeons?
 Yes. Assistant surgeon claims are billed with modifier 80, and co-surgeon cases where two surgeons perform distinct parts of one procedure use modifier 62, though eligibility rules vary by payer and procedure. These claims are frequently denied on technicalities without proper documentation.
Q6: Why do general surgery claims get denied?
 General surgery claims are most often denied for global period modifier errors, missing documentation on assistant or co-surgeon claims, and incomplete coding of multiple procedures performed in one operation. Reconciling every claim against the full operative report prevents most of these denials.
