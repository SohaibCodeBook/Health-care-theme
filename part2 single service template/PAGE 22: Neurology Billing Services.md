 H2: Neurology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is neurology billing?
 Neurology billing is the coding and claims process for neurology practices, covering diagnostic studies like EEG and EMG, Botox and infusion therapies, and prolonged service codes for long, complex visits. It requires expertise in study-specific coding and the prior authorizations attached to neurological biologics.
Q2: Why should neurology practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing neurology billing gives your practice specialists in EEG and EMG coding, Botox claims, and prolonged visit capture. Neurologists deliver long, complex encounters plus technical diagnostics, and specialized billing makes sure both the cognitive work and the studies are fully reimbursed.
Q3: How much do neurology billing services cost?
 Neurology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections. Because undercounted EMG nerves and unclaimed prolonged service time are common, specialized billing usually recovers well beyond the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: How are EMG and nerve conduction studies coded?
 EMG and nerve conduction studies are coded based on the number of nerves tested and muscles or extremities examined, with correct component modifiers applied for who owns the equipment and who interprets the study. Undercounted nerves are a routine cause of neurology underpayment.
Q5: How is Botox billed for chronic migraine?
 Botox billing for chronic migraine combines the injection procedure code with drug units documented against the vial and dose administered, along with the prior authorization the therapy requires. Drug unit errors on Botox claims are expensive, so unit accuracy matters on every claim.
Q6: Why do neurology claims get denied?
 Neurology claims are most often denied for undercounted diagnostic study units, missing component modifiers, and lapsed prior authorizations on CGRP biologics or MS infusions. Because neurology visits run long and complex, missing prolonged service documentation is also a common source of underpayment.
