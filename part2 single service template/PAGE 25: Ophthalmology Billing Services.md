 H2: Ophthalmology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is ophthalmology billing?
 Ophthalmology billing is the coding and claims process for eye care practices, covering a distinct eye code family alongside standard E/M codes, intravitreal injections, cataract surgery, and diagnostic testing. It requires choosing the higher-paying code path between eye codes and E/M on every visit.
Q2: Why should ophthalmology practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing ophthalmology billing gives your practice specialists in eye codes, injection billing, and cataract claim rules. Because ophthalmology has its own code family alongside E/M, specialized billers choose the better-paying path on every visit while generic billers default to one and lose the difference.
Q3: How much do ophthalmology billing services cost?
 Ophthalmology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections. Because code selection errors and injection unit mistakes are common and recurring, specialized billing usually recovers well beyond its cost. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: What is the difference between eye codes and E/M codes?
 Eye visit codes and E/M codes have different documentation requirements and payer reimbursement rates, and the better-paying choice varies by payer and by the specific visit documentation. Comparing both paths on every encounter and billing the code the documentation supports at the higher rate is standard practice.
Q5: How are intravitreal injections billed?
 Intravitreal injections are billed with the injection procedure code plus the drug's J-code units, correct laterality, and any payer step therapy requirements for drugs like Eylea, Lucentis, or Avastin. Retina injections are high-volume, high-dollar recurring claims, so drug unit accuracy matters on every one.
Q6: How does cataract co-management billing work?
 Cataract co-management splits the global period billing between the surgeon and the co-managing optometrist using modifiers 54 and 55, with transfer documentation required by payers. Clean co-management billing protects both revenue and referral relationships.
