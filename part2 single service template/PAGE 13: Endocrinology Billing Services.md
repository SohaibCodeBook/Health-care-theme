Section 6: FAQs
Eyebrow: FAQS
 H2: Endocrinology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is endocrinology billing?
 Endocrinology billing is the coding and claims process for practices treating diabetes, thyroid disease, and metabolic disorders, covering complex E/M visits, CGM and device billing, and chronic care management programs. It requires documentation that reflects the medical decision making complexity of managing multiple chronic conditions.
Q2: Why should endocrinology practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing endocrinology billing gives your practice specialists in complex E/M coding, CGM and device claims, and chronic care management capture. Endocrinologists manage some of the most complex patients in outpatient medicine, and specialized billing makes sure reimbursement finally matches that complexity.
Q3: How much do endocrinology billing services cost?
 Endocrinology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections. Because chronic care management and CGM interpretation are commonly delivered but rarely billed, recovered revenue from these alone often exceeds the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: Is continuous glucose monitor interpretation billable?
 Yes. CGM placement, patient training, and data interpretation are separately billable services under specific CPT codes, with different rules for personal versus professional devices. This is a recurring revenue stream many endocrinology practices deliver but never consistently bill.
Q5: Can endocrinology practices bill for chronic care management?
 Yes. Diabetic and thyroid patients with multiple chronic conditions often qualify for chronic care management and remote monitoring programs that reimburse monthly for care coordination work already being performed. Most endocrinology practices leave this revenue uncaptured.
Q6: Why do endocrinology visits get undercoded?
 Endocrinology visits get undercoded when documentation does not clearly reflect the medical decision making involved in managing multiple interacting chronic conditions, or when billers default to conservative middle-tier E/M levels. Coding to the actual complexity documented is the fix.
