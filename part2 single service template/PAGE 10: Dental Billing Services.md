Eyebrow: FAQS
 H2: Dental billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is dental billing?
 Dental billing is the process of coding and submitting insurance claims for dental procedures using CDT codes, distinct from the CPT codes used in medical billing. It requires attaching x-rays and narratives, tracking frequency limitations and annual maximums, and understanding downgrade policies unique to dental insurance.
Q2: Why should dental practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing dental billing gives your practice specialists in CDT coding, claim attachments, and insurance follow-up. Most dental offices that outsource see their aging A/R shrink, their front desk freed to focus on patients, and fewer claims written off because nobody had time to appeal them.
Q3: How much do dental billing services cost?
 Dental billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections, varying by claim volume and the level of aging A/R cleanup needed. Because missing attachments and unworked aging claims are common, recovered revenue usually exceeds the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: Why do dental claims get denied?
 Dental claims are most often denied for missing attachments like x-rays or narratives, exceeded frequency limits, unmet waiting periods, and missing tooth clause exclusions. Submitting complete documentation with every claim the first time prevents the majority of dental denials.
Q5: What is medical-dental cross coding?
 Medical-dental cross coding bills procedures like oral surgery, sleep appliances, and trauma-related dental work to medical insurance using CPT and diagnosis codes instead of, or alongside, dental insurance. Many dental offices never pursue this reimbursement even though qualifying procedures happen regularly.
Q6: How do you verify dental insurance benefits before an appointment?
 Insurance verification involves confirming annual maximums, frequency limits, waiting periods, and missing tooth clauses directly with the payer before the patient's visit. Accurate verification prevents surprise denials and lets your team present treatment costs to patients with confidence.
