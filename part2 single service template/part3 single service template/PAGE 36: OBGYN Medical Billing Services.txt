 H2: OBGYN billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is OBGYN medical billing?
 OBGYN medical billing is the coding and claims process for obstetric and gynecologic practices, covering global maternity packages, ultrasound imaging, well-woman visits, and gynecologic surgery. It requires unbundling maternity care correctly when patients transfer or deliver unexpectedly, since obstetric billing follows different rules than standard visit-based specialties.
Q2: Why should OBGYN practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing OBGYN billing gives your practice specialists in global maternity billing, ultrasound frequency rules, and well-woman visit coding. Because OBGYN blends bundled obstetric packages with high-frequency imaging and surgical gynecology, specialized billing captures revenue that generic billers miss when care does not fit the standard package.
Q3: How much do OBGYN billing services cost?
 OBGYN billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections. Because global maternity unbundling errors and undercounted ultrasounds are common, recovered revenue often exceeds the fee. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: How does global maternity billing work?
 Global maternity billing bundles routine antepartum care, delivery, and postpartum care into a single package code, but care must be unbundled and billed separately when a patient transfers practices mid-pregnancy, delivers with a different provider, or receives incomplete care. Billing the global package when care was incomplete is a common OBGYN revenue leak.
Q5: Can a well-woman visit and a problem visit be billed on the same day?
 Yes, when documentation supports a separately identifiable problem addressed during the preventive well-woman exam, using modifier 25 to bill both. OBGYN practices routinely give away problem visits inside annual exams when this modifier is not applied or the documentation split is unclear.
Q6: Why do OBGYN claims get denied?
 OBGYN claims are most often denied for global maternity package errors when care was not complete, ultrasounds billed past frequency or medical necessity limits, and missing modifier 25 on combined well-woman and problem visits. Because obstetric care spans many months and providers, reconciling the full pregnancy episode against what was billed prevents most of these denials.
