 H2: Gastroenterology billing questions we hear most
Q1: What is gastroenterology billing?
 Gastroenterology billing is the coding and claims process for GI practices, covering endoscopy procedures, screening versus diagnostic colonoscopy rules, and infusion billing for IBD therapies. It requires correctly sequencing multiple endoscopy procedures and applying screening modifiers that protect patient cost-sharing.
Q2: Why should GI practices outsource their billing?
 Outsourcing gastroenterology billing gives your practice specialists in endoscopy coding, screening modifier rules, and multiple procedure logic. GI revenue is concentrated in procedures, so a small error rate on scopes costs more than in visit-based specialties, and specialized billing protects that revenue.
Q3: How much do gastroenterology billing services cost?
 Gastroenterology billing services typically charge a percentage of monthly collections, reflecting the high value of endoscopic procedures. Because screening modifier and bundling errors are common and expensive, specialized billing usually recovers more than it costs. Request a free billing analysis to see what your practice could recover.
Q4: What happens when a screening colonoscopy becomes diagnostic?
 When a screening colonoscopy includes polyp removal, modifier 33 or PT is applied so the claim keeps its screening status and the patient keeps their preventive cost-sharing protection. Getting this wrong creates surprise patient bills and is one of the most common GI billing complaints.
Q5: How are multiple procedures in one endoscopy session billed?
 Multiple endoscopy procedures performed in the same session are sequenced under payer payment rules so the highest-valued procedure pays in full and related procedures pay correctly rather than being bundled away entirely. Biopsies, polypectomies, and dilations each have specific capture rules.
Q6: Why do gastroenterology claims get denied?
 GI claims are most often denied for incorrect screening versus diagnostic coding, multiple procedure bundling errors, and missing prior authorizations on infusion therapies for IBD. Because these errors also generate patient billing complaints, preventing them protects both revenue and patient relationships.
